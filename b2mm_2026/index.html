About

Three-dimensional renders of real B0 → μ+μ− candidates recorded by LHCb in 2024 and 2025, plus one clearly labelled simulation. Every element is placed from the recorded data and the official detector geometry; no image generation is involved.

All files are 6000 × 4000 pixels at 300 dpi, suitable for print. Please credit them as:

LHCb Collaboration / Federico Leo Redi

Real candidates

Each event passes the B0 → μ+μ− analysis selection. A single candidate can still be background, so call it a candidate decay. Each comes in three versions: without text, with a labelled close-up of the collision point, and on a white background.

Colours

  • Pink. The two muons from the B0 decay, and the muon-chamber pads they hit.
  • Blue. Other charged particles from the same proton–proton collision.
  • Amber. Energy in the electromagnetic calorimeter.
  • Teal. Energy in the hadronic calorimeter and hits in the muon chambers (2025 event).
  • Faint outlines. The LHCb detector, drawn lightly so the muons stand out.

Provenance

Data

  • Candidates and muon track states come from the B0 → μ+μ− analysis (event package prepared by Harry Cliff). The B0 vertex is the analysis refit. The B0 momentum is the sum of the two muon momenta from the track fit, and the decay time is flight distance × mass / momentum.
  • 2025 event: calorimeter cells and all muon hits decoded from the raw data stored with the event, using the live event display’s own tools. For the most energetic clusters the summed cell energies agree with the trigger’s own reconstruction to within about 5%.
  • 2024 events: only the electromagnetic-calorimeter clusters reconstructed by the trigger are stored, so there is no hadronic calorimeter or full muon-hit information.

Detector and drawing

  • Detector: the official LHCb geometry (DD4hep, 2025 version, as used by the Phoenix event display).
  • Lines between measured points are interpolated for display, and the other tracks get a single bend in the magnet.
  • Rendered with three.js from the numbers alone. The rendering code was written with an AI coding assistant; no AI image generation is involved.

Using the images

  • Use the suggested caption or keep its key words: candidate for the real events, simulated or visualisation for the simulation.
