Research interests

I am an experimental particle physicist. With experiments at CERN I look for physics beyond the Standard Model (the theory we use today for the elementary particles and their forces). What we are after: something that tells us what dark matter is, or what neutrinos really are.

I also like to take the computational methods we develop for particle physics and use them somewhere else: medical imaging, environmental science, and the physics of climate change.

And I work on detectors: how to design them, how to optimise them, and how to use machine learning to make them perform better. Some of this is for experiments that do not exist yet.

Background

I studied physics at Imperial College London and stayed there for my PhD. After that: postdoc at EPFL in Lausanne, Senior Research Fellow at CERN, and now the University of Bergamo. Over the years I worked on the LHCb, SHiP and SND@LHC experiments.

Teaching

I teach fundamental physics at the University of Bergamo. My current and recent teaching includes electromagnetism and the physics of climate change, taught as compulsory courses to large undergraduate classes in Engineering Science. If you are interested for MSc or PhD work please just email me. Remember the Italian PhD schools open in cycles.

Outreach

I do outreach since my PhD, and I still enjoy it a lot. I started as a moderator for the International Particle Physics Masterclasses (high-school students analysing real LHC data for a day), then I became head of the programme for my collaboration. Now I am also on the steering board of IPPOG, the International Particle Physics Outreach Group.

I also collaborated with several radio programmes of Rai, the Italian public broadcaster: for example Caterpillar on Rai Radio 2.
